# Content Moderation — Decisioning Policy

**Owner:** Trust & Safety

Every flagged post is resolved as **Approve** (left up), **Hold** (unresolved — the content stays suppressed pending a document, verification, or decision), or **Reject** (removed). A corroborated problem warrants a Reject; a real but unresolved question warrants a Hold; absent either, the post is Approved.

## Weighing & proportionality

Signals are weighed against exposure and track record, not applied mechanically, and the response is proportionate to what is at stake. A minor anomaly, or an immaterial reach, on an otherwise sound and well-established creator does not warrant holding the post; the same signal against a large live audience, or against a new account with little history to stand on, may. A long, consistent, verified history is mitigating — a new account has not yet earned that benefit of the doubt. No single small factor need be decisive, yet several unexplained concerns together can be enough to treat a post as unresolved. Some level of reports, filtered posts, and retractions is normal; what matters is deviation from what the creator's own history and profile would lead us to expect. Where genuine doubt remains and reach is exposed, holding is preferred to releasing.

## Blocklist & identity match

Blocklist exposure carries zero tolerance: a genuine blocklist match is disqualifying. Blocklist screening is alias-based and routinely produces false positives; a hit that does not hold up as the same individual is not, in itself, a concern.

## Author linkage

A genuine connection to known abuse — one that ties the same party to a bad account — is a serious concern. Incidental overlaps that don't establish common control are not, and neither is a connection to the creator's own accounts in good standing.

## Posting activity

Activity is judged on pattern, not volume. A spam burst — many near-identical short posts pushed through many distinct sockpuppet origins in a short window, mostly filtered, little actually served — is itself the abuse; and the rapid build-up of an audience followed by mass link-drops to an unestablished destination (a farm-and-dump) is abuse too. In a farm-and-dump the harm is realised when the links go out, so it does not depend on user reports to confirm it. Ordinary posting, however voluminous or fast-growing, is not a concern.

## Identity, control & reach at risk

Where the party — or their control of the account — cannot presently be established and reach is exposed, the risk is unresolved rather than confirmed.

## Confirmed history

A confirmed, adjudicated problem against the same party is disqualifying. A problem is only "confirmed" when a real prior determination stands behind it; a flag with nothing substantiating it, or one that conflicts with the record, is a data-quality question, not a confirmed problem.

## Evidence

Conflicting sources are weighed on their merits. A creator's own account of events is not evidence on its own.
